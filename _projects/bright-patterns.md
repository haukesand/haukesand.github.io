Dark patterns have a name, a taxonomy, and a regulatory audience. Their opposite did not. In a CHI '23 workshop paper {% cite sandhaus2023promoting %} I proposed bright patterns as benevolent design solutions that prioritize user goals and well-being, even where that works against a company's short-term interest. Brightpatterns.org is the public front page for that term.

The site has three parts. A Hall of Fame of fourteen named patterns, such as Nutrition Label, Escape Hatch, Slow Down, and Usage Limits, each with a description and real examples. A Discoveries in the Wild section for patterns people spot in products. And a submission form, so the collection grows through nominations rather than only through my own reading.

The site also carries a citation block for people who use the term in papers or policy work. I update it when new submissions arrive and when my own work on UX evaluation metrics {% cite Sandhaus2026-indecent %} shifts how I think about the taxonomy.
