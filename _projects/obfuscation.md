Obfuscation is the deliberate addition of ambiguous, confusing, or misleading information to interfere with surveillance and data collection. Finn Brunton and Helen Nissenbaum wrote the book on it in 2015. Obfuscation.tech is our attempt to make the practice usable for people who are not security researchers.

The premise is that the communication channel cannot be trusted. Headsets, wearables, drones, robotaxis, and social media platforms all feed data to a small number of AI operators and data hoarders, and notice-and-consent does nothing about it. Rather than asking users to opt out, the site documents concrete strategies they can apply themselves, such as fonts that keep website text readable for humans but useless for scrapers, or ways to keep workplace tools from recording exactly how much and how fast you work. Each strategy is tagged by topic and by what it protects against.

The site is young and still sparse. It is built to be edited: every page has an edit link, and contributions are welcome. If you have a strategy that works, add it.
